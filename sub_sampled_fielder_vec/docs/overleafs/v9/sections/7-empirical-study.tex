We test both sampling rates in two complementary regimes. In the \emph{synthesized} regime the similarity matrix $S$ is built directly from the closed-form constant-block CFBM (\Cref{asm:cfbm}), isolating the linear algebra with no estimation noise. In the \emph{generated} regime dendropy simulates Kingman coalescent trees and evolves JC69 sequences of length $\ell=10^4$, from which $S$ is the log-det similarity (\Cref{asm:gtr}); this probes whether the two hypotheses the proof actually rests on --- the exact cross-clan baseline of \Cref{asm:clock} and the separation margin of \Cref{asm:margin} --- survive realistic tree shapes and finite-sequence noise. The intra-clan structure that the constant-block CFBM idealizes away is not at issue: \eqref{eq:cfbm_floor} leaves it free. Each regime is then run through both operators of \Cref{sec:problem}: the Laplacian $L$ of \Cref{thm:main-sim}, and the centered distance operator $\Bop=H\Dmat H$ of \Cref{thm:main-dist}. Each rounds as its own theorem does --- $k$-means on the normalized Laplacian $L_{\mathrm{sym}}$ for the similarity route, the sign pattern of $\uone$ for the distance route.

Recovery is scored by the Normalized Mutual Information (NMI) of the recovered split $\hat C$ against the planted split $C^\ast$. NMI is the standard accuracy metric for evaluating spectral-clustering partitions against a known ground truth \cite{fortunato2010community, qinrohe2013regularized}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nmi_def}
   \mathrm{NMI}(Y,C)=\frac{2\,I(Y;C)}{H(Y)+H(C)},
\end{equation}
with $I(Y;C)$ the mutual information and $H(\cdot)$ the Shannon entropy. NMI attains its maximum value $1$ if and only if the two partitions are identical up to relabeling, and decays as they disagree \cite{strehlghosh2002cluster, fortunato2010community}. Composing this with the sign-recovery guarantee of \Cref{thm:main-sim} fixes the population endpoint of our sweeps.

\begin{corollary}[Perfect partition recovery and $\mathrm{NMI}=1$]\label{cor:nmi}
Under the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:main-sim}, the spectral partition $\hat C=\{\hat C_A,\hat C_B\}$ obtained by thresholding $\vvh$ at zero satisfies $\hat C=C^\ast$ \hp, and therefore $\mathrm{NMI}(\hat C,C^\ast)=1$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
\Cref{thm:main-sim} guarantees $\norm{\vvh-\vv}_\infty<\min_i|\vv_i|$, so for each node $i$, $|\vvh_i-\vv_i|<|\vv_i|$, which forces $\vvh_i$ and $\vv_i$ to share a sign. Under the CFBM the population Fiedler vector is sign-constant on each clan \cite{aizenbud2023spectral} ($\vv_i>0$ on $A$, $\vv_i<0$ on $B$), so the zero-threshold partition of $\vvh$ recovers $C^\ast$ exactly. Exact recovery gives $I(\hat C;C^\ast)=H(\hat C)=H(C^\ast)$, whence by \eqref{eq:nmi_def} $\mathrm{NMI}(\hat C,C^\ast)=1$.
\end{proof}

All four figures below share one layout: one pair of panels per imbalance, at $\eta=1,5,10,15$ throughout. On the left, measured NMI against the sampling rate $p$, one curve per size, the curves running dark to light (viridis) with increasing size, and the red dashed line at the $0.90$ level that defines the empirical threshold $\hat p^\star$. On the right, $\hat p^\star$ against size on log-log axes, with a dotted reference of the shape the governing rate predicts --- $\log m/m$, from \eqref{eq:main_rate} on the similarity side and \eqref{eq:main-rate-dist-balanced} on the distance side --- fitted to the measured points. Filled markers are measured; open markers are bounds rather than thresholds, a read-off pinned at either end of the $p$ grid, and are excluded from the fit.

Along the similarity route the transition sharpens and shifts left as the size grows, and $\hat p^\star$ decays parallel to the reference at every $\eta$ in both regimes: with no estimation noise in \Cref{fig:pstar_synth}, and on Kingman trees in \Cref{fig:pstar_gen}, where the coherence and gap identities of \Cref{prop:coherence} and \Cref{lem:gap} continue to hold despite off-model tree shapes and JC69 noise (\Cref{asm:gtr}). The $\Theta(\log n/n)$ sample complexity predicted by \eqref{eq:main_rate} therefore survives realistic finite-sequence estimation noise.

Run through $\Bop$ on the same data, the sign rule of \Cref{thm:main-dist} reproduces that decay at $\eta=1$ and degrades progressively away from balance, until at $\eta=15$ most synthesized sizes never recover below full data (\Cref{fig:pstar_synth_dist,fig:pstar_gen_dist}). Only the $\eta=1$ panel of \Cref{fig:pstar_synth_dist} lies inside the configuration where hypotheses (i)--(ii) of \Cref{thm:main-dist} are verified (\Cref{cor:dist-balanced}); the remaining panels measure the estimator rather than certify the rate. Which operator tolerates more noise is not read off these sweeps --- the two routes differ in rounding rule as well as in operator --- and \Cref{rem:gap-scope} compares the two rates' parameter groups, not the two estimators.
